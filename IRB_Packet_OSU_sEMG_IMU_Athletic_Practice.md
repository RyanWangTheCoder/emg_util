Dear OSU HRPP / IRB,

I am submitting a new application for a minimal-risk pilot case-series study using passive wearable surface electromyography (sEMG) and inertial measurement unit (IMU) sensors during ordinary athletic practice. One participant collects sensor data during tennis practice at a residential court; the other collects sensor data during running at a neighborhood fitness center. The two participants are minor student-athletes who are also intended student co-authors on the **future IEEE-targeted manuscripts** that this work supports. The study collects sEMG, IMU, and simple subjective fatigue/exertion/pain ratings; it collects no video, no audio, no heart-rate, no SpO₂, and no other clinical measures, and it does not direct the participants to exceed their normal training intensity.

Only data collected **after IRB approval** will be used to support these future IEEE submissions; any pre-IRB engineering test data and any non-IEEE-targeted prior write-ups are out of scope of this protocol.

The study is designed as **non-exempt minimal-risk research** under [45 CFR 46.404](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-A/part-46/subpart-D/section-46.404) and is anticipated eligible for **expedited review** under [45 CFR 46.110](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-A/part-46/subpart-A/section-46.110) as minimal-risk research involving passive, noninvasive physiological sensor recording and analysis of movement and behavior during ordinary athletic practice. **OSU will determine the applicable expedited category.** It is conducted in conformance with the U.S. Common Rule, 45 CFR 46 Subpart D, and the [Declaration of Helsinki](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-helsinki-ethical-principles-for-medical-research-involving-human-subjects/).

### 3. Study Purpose

The purpose of this study is to evaluate the feasibility and field validity of passive wearable forearm sEMG and IMU monitoring during athletic practice in two minor student-athletes who are also student researcher/co-authors on the project. One participant (Ryan) is recorded during tennis practice; the other (Theodore) is recorded during running at a neighborhood fitness center. The study examines forearm sEMG features and motion context across these two activities, and documents field-deployment factors such as electrode settling, contact quality, motion artifacts, and session continuity across two distinct off-site settings.

The study is **not** intended to diagnose, treat, prevent, or predict any medical condition or injury. The system is used only for non-clinical research signal recording and engineering analysis, and no clinical decisions are made from the data; the study does not test a therapeutic or diagnostic device claim. The PI will address any OSU questions about FDA/device applicability if raised.

### 4. Study Design

This is a prospective, minimal-risk, two-participant pilot **case series** under [45 CFR 46.404](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-A/part-46/subpart-D/section-46.404). Only data collected after IRB approval will be used for research analysis, conference submission, or publication.

Each participant will complete approximately **5 to 10 sessions**, each up to approximately **90 minutes** (including setup, warmup, the participant's usual athletic activity, brief subjective ratings, and sensor removal). The study does not require exercise beyond the participant's usual training. Participants are not instructed to exercise to exhaustion or to perform maximal-effort fatigue protocols. Rest breaks may be taken at any time.

The two-participant, two-activity design is motivated scientifically by the need for **cross-activity diversification** of the field-validity claims to be evaluated in future IEEE-targeted manuscript(s): tennis (Ryan) and running (Theodore) place distinct mechanical and physiological demands on the forearm, and recording both supports more general signal-quality and IMU-context observations than a single activity could. The two distinct off-site settings also test deployment robustness across different settings.

### 11. Study Procedures

#### 11.1 Pre-session safety check

At the start of each session, the on-site supervising adult and (where applicable) the IAA confirm that:

- The participant still agrees to participate.
- The participant has no pain, injury, skin irritation, or feeling that would make participation in his named athletic activity inadvisable.
- Court, facility, equipment, and weather conditions, as applicable, are safe.
- The participant is reminded that he may rest or stop at any time.

#### 11.2 Sensor placement

A passive surface EMG sensor (MyoWare 2.0) is placed on the skin over the forearm muscle region of interest — typically the wrist flexor and/or wrist extensor region. A reference/ground electrode is placed at an appropriate bony landmark (e.g., near the lateral epicondyle), consistent with vendor guidance and PI review. A passive IMU motion sensor (ICM-20948) is secured on the forearm using a strap or other non-invasive support.

For tennis (Ryan), this region is heavily activated during racket grip and swings. For running (Theodore), the same placement records lower-amplitude forearm activity that serves as a contrasting motion regime for the analysis; placement, recording, and removal procedures are otherwise identical.

The sensor kit is **passive only**. It records naturally occurring muscle electrical activity and motion. It does **not** stimulate the muscle, deliver medication, deliver heat, or provide treatment. See §32 (Device Safety Statement) and Doc 5 for the full device description.

#### 11.3 Baseline / warmup recording

A short baseline recording may be collected at rest and during light movements (e.g., gentle wrist motion, racket holding, warmup strokes). This baseline supports signal-quality assessment and later analysis only; it is not a medical evaluation.

#### 11.4 Activity recording

Activity is recorded during the participant's usual training routine. The study does not prescribe additional training load beyond regular practice.

- **Site A — Ryan (tennis):** rallying, serves, forehands, backhands, volleys, footwork, and ordinary practice-match play.
- **Site B — Theodore (running):** indoor running on the fitness-center treadmill or other running surface available at the facility; warmup and cooldown as part of his usual fitness routine. For treadmill running, the participant follows standard facility safety procedures, uses the treadmill emergency-stop mechanism where available, and may stop or reduce speed at any time.

#### 11.5 Subjective ratings

At the end of each session — or at selected time points if approved by the IRB — the participant may be asked to provide simple subjective ratings on 0–10 scales:

- **Forearm fatigue:** 0 = none, 10 = maximum.
- **Overall exertion:** 0 = none, 10 = maximum.
- **Pain/discomfort:** 0 = none, 10 = maximum.

If the participant reports pain or unusual discomfort, the session is stopped and the parent is notified if not already present.

#### 11.6 Sensor removal and skin check

After activity, sensors are removed. The participant is asked whether there is skin irritation, discomfort, or other concern. If irritation occurs, the site is monitored and the parent is informed.

### 12. Data Collected

- Forearm sEMG signals from passive surface electrodes (MyoWare 2.0 ENV + RAW outputs).
- Forearm motion data from a passive IMU (accelerometer/gyroscope; ICM-20948).
- Session timing and activity labels (e.g., tennis practice, rally, serve, practice match; running session; warmup/cooldown), if recorded.
- Subjective fatigue, exertion, and pain/discomfort ratings on 0–10 scales.
- Minimal contextual information needed for analysis: session date, approximate duration, dominant arm, activity type (tennis or running), and general activity context.

The study **does not** collect heart rate, SpO₂, blood pressure, ECG, or any other vital-sign data; **does not** record video or audio; and **does not** collect biospecimens.

### 13. Data Analysis Plan

The research team analyzes sEMG features such as RMS amplitude, median frequency (MDF), mean frequency (MNF), and signal quality / contact-related metrics. IMU data is used to identify swings, estimate movement intensity, and provide motion context for interpreting EMG changes. Subjective ratings may be compared with sensor-derived features. Analyses are descriptive and exploratory because the study involves only two participants.

The study **avoids clinical claims**. Results are described as feasibility, field-validity, signal-quality, or fatigue-consistent observations — not diagnosis, treatment, or injury prediction.

### 14. Risks and Mitigation

The expected overall risk level is **minimal**. The main risks are mild skin irritation, ordinary athletic fatigue/soreness, privacy or identifiability risk, and potential undue influence due to the family/co-authorship structure of the study team. A detailed risk assessment is provided in **Doc 4 — Risk Assessment**; mitigations are summarized there and in §10, §16, §22, and §23.

#### 14.1 Ordinary athletic risk vs. research-added risk

The IRB should distinguish ordinary athletic risk (which exists with or without this study) from research-added risk (which exists because of this study).

- **Ordinary athletic risk** includes muscle soreness, fatigue, falls, and minor injury inherent to the activity itself — tennis-specific risks for Ryan, and running/fitness-equipment risks for Theodore. The research does **not** require participants to exceed their normal training intensity.
- **Research-added risk** is limited to: possible mild skin irritation from adhesive electrodes/tape/straps; minor inconvenience from sensor wear; and confidentiality/identifiability risk because this is a small named cohort.

### 15. Benefits

There is **no guaranteed direct benefit** to either participant. Possible indirect benefits include educational experience in wearable sensing, signal processing, and responsible human-subjects research. The study may contribute to improved understanding of field-deployable sports sensing methods.

## Document 5 — Sensor and Device Description

This document describes the sEMG/IMU sensor kit used in the study so that OSU IRB reviewers can evaluate the device's risk profile without reference to any technical manuscript.

### 5.1 Hardware overview

The system is a low-cost wearable EMG/IMU node that records signals only. It does not stimulate, deliver medication, deliver heat, or perform any therapeutic or diagnostic function.

| Component | Specification | Function |
|---|---|---|
| MyoWare 2.0 muscle sensor | 2000 Hz sampling, 12-bit ADC, ENV (envelope) and RAW outputs | Passive surface EMG recording from forearm muscle group |
| ICM-20948 IMU | 200 Hz, ±2000 dps gyro, ±8 g accelerometer | Passive forearm motion recording (no body stimulation) |
| ESP32 MCU | BLE-streaming microcontroller | Sample acquisition and wireless data transmission |
| Adhesive snap electrodes | Disposable Ag/AgCl, skin-safe | Electrical contact between skin and EMG sensor |
| Reference electrode | Same type, placed near lateral epicondyle | Reference/ground for the EMG amplifier |
| Battery | Lithium-polymer, low-voltage | Powers the entire kit. **No mains-electrical connection during use.** |

Approximate kit cost: ~$99 in components; the kit is **personally owned** (not OSU lab equipment).

### 5.2 Signal-only operation

The system is **passive recording only**:

- The MyoWare 2.0 records naturally occurring muscle electrical activity from the skin surface. It does **not** deliver electrical stimulation.
- The ICM-20948 records motion only. It does not interact with the body in any way.
- The ESP32 MCU only streams data over BLE. It does not affect the participant's body.
- The system delivers **no** medication, heat, vibration, current, or therapeutic intervention.

### 5.3 Software pipeline

| Module | Function |
|---|---|
| `btCentral.py` | BLE central (Python/Bleak) — receives binary TLV frames, writes rotating CSV logs |
| `capture_loader.py` | Reconstructs hybrid `abs_us` timeline; uses device timestamps within a connection and host receive-time gaps between reconnections |
| `emg_util_yaml_runner.py` | Per-swing MDF/MNF/RMS feature extraction and reporting |
| Sampling architecture | ADC DMA at 40 kHz with 10× oversampling; ±25 µs timestamp accuracy at 2000 Hz |

Software runs on personal computers controlled by the study team; data is written to encrypted local storage or OSU-licensed storage. See Doc 7.

### 5.4 Wear, attachment, removal

- Surface EMG electrodes are placed on the skin over the wrist flexor and/or wrist extensor region of the forearm. A reference electrode is placed near the lateral epicondyle.
- The IMU is secured on the forearm with a non-invasive strap.
- The kit is removable at any time; participants may remove sensors themselves.
- Sessions are stopped immediately on discomfort, irritation, dizziness, pain, or request.

### 5.5 Regulatory framing

- The system is used only for non-clinical research signal recording and engineering analysis.
- Not used for diagnosis, treatment, prevention, monitoring of a medical condition, or any clinical decision-making.
- The study does not test a therapeutic or diagnostic device claim. The PI will address any OSU questions about FDA/device applicability if raised, rather than asserting a categorical "not a medical device" determination upfront.
- No medical interpretation, diagnosis, training advice, or individualized health recommendation is provided to participants or parents based on the data.

## Document 7 — Data Security Plan

### 7.1 Data classification

Per OSU's data-classification framework, the data collected under this protocol are anticipated **Level 2 (Confidential)**: identifiable or coded data with minimal confidentiality risk if breached. Level 3 (High) does not apply because no PHI, financial, or regulated identifiers are collected.

### 7.2 N=2 indirect-identifiability acknowledgment

Because the cohort is N=2 and both participants may be named as manuscript authors, **raw and coded physiological data are treated as potentially identifiable even after removal of direct identifiers**. Public sharing of raw participant-level data is **not** permitted under this protocol, and any IEEE data-availability statement reflects this constraint.

### 7.3 Data flow

```
[On-site ESP32 + sensors]
        │ BLE short-range session transmission
        │ (no direct identifiers in payload; link-layer encryption if configured)
        ▼
[Personal laptop running btCentral.py]
        │ TLS where applicable; local file write
        ▼
[Encrypted local storage OR OSU-licensed storage]
        │
        ├─► [Identity-key file: SEPARATE encrypted storage, restricted access]
        │
        └─► [Coded research data: feature extraction + analysis]
                                │
                                ▼
                       [De-identified aggregate metrics → manuscript]
```

### 7.4 Storage controls

| Control | Implementation |
|---|---|
| Storage location | Encrypted local drive on study-team computer, **or** OSU-licensed cloud storage (e.g., OSU OneDrive/Box). **No** non-OSU-licensed cloud storage. |
| Encryption at rest | Full-disk encryption (BitLocker, FileVault, or LUKS) on study computers. Identity-key file additionally encrypted with a separate passphrase. |
| Encryption in transit | The BLE link between sensor and laptop is short-range and used only during a session. If pairing/link-layer encryption is configured on the ESP32 firmware, it is enabled; the primary confidentiality control is immediate write to encrypted local or OSU-licensed storage, and **no direct identifiers are transmitted in the BLE payload** (only sensor codes and signal data). TLS is used for any cloud sync to OSU-licensed storage. |
| Access control | Limited to named study-team members; OSU-licensed systems use OSU SSO; local storage protected by OS user accounts. |
| Identity-key separation | Identity-to-code mapping stored in a separate encrypted file from the research data. |
| OS hygiene | Patched OS; current antivirus; software firewall enabled; disk-level encryption enabled. |
| Backup | Routine encrypted backup; backups follow the same access and encryption controls. |

### 7.5 Retention and destruction

- **Retention period:** coded research data retained for **[N] years** (final value TBD by PI before submission; **recommended: 5 years after publication or study closure** unless OSU has another requirement) after publication or study closure.
- **Identity-key destruction:** the identity-to-code mapping is destroyed earlier, when no longer needed for the manuscript or any IRB-approved follow-up.
- **De-identified aggregate metrics:** may be retained longer with IRB approval if needed for academic recordkeeping.
- **Disposal:** secure cryptographic erasure at end of retention.

### 7.6 Sharing

- **Raw data:** not publicly shared. Not provided to non-team members without IRB amendment.
- **Manuscript-grade summary metrics:** may be shared with IEEE editors/reviewers under journal confidentiality, and with the corresponding author on reasonable request, subject to the IRB-approved data-sharing limits. **Requests will be reviewed by the PI and fulfilled only if consistent with the IRB-approved protocol and participant confidentiality (e.g., no re-identification risk introduced through aggregation choices).**

### 7.7 Breach reporting

A lost device, lost storage media, unauthorized access, or any other breach of the data security plan is reported as an adverse event per Appx C. OSU HRPP and (if Level 2 cloud storage was involved) OSU Information Security are notified per OSU policy.

## Appendix C — Adverse Event and Continuing Review Procedure

### C.1 Definitions

- **Adverse event (AE):** any untoward physical, psychological, or social occurrence experienced by a participant during or after a study session, whether or not the study team believes the event was caused by the research.
- **Protocol deviation:** any departure from the IRB-approved protocol, intentional or unintentional.
- **Reportable new information (RNI):** any AE, deviation, or other event that meets OSU HRPP definitions of unanticipated problems involving risks to participants or others.

### C.2 Reportable events (non-exhaustive list)

- Skin redness lasting beyond a session, rash, or blistering from electrodes/tape/straps.
- Pain or unusual discomfort from device placement.
- Privacy breach, lost device, or lost data.
- Unauthorized access to study storage.
- Distress or perceived pressure to participate (whether reported by the participant, the IAA, or another team member).
- Any athletic injury occurring during a research-recorded session — tennis-related or running/fitness-related, even if likely ordinary sport-related — is logged and evaluated.
- Any IAA-initiated session halt under §19 of Doc 1.

### C.3 Reporting timeline

- **Serious AE or unanticipated problem:** reported to OSU HRPP through Cayuse HE within the OSU-required timeline (typically 5 business days; PI verifies current value at submission).
- **Routine AE / minor deviation:** logged in the study record and summarized at continuing review.
- **Privacy/data breach:** immediately to OSU HRPP and, if Level 2 cloud storage was involved, OSU Information Security.

### C.4 Continuing review and amendments

- Continuing review per OSU HRPP timeline as stated in the IRB approval letter (typically annual for non-exempt studies).
- Amendments for any material change to procedures, study team, sites, sensor configuration, or risk profile are submitted **before** taking effect.
- Adult-consent transition for each participant is submitted as an amendment if data collection is anticipated to continue past the 18th birthday.

### C.5 Records retained

- AE log: date, description, response, outcome, reporter.
- Deviation log: same fields.
- IAA contact log: any session halts, re-affirmation discussions, or participant-reported concerns.
