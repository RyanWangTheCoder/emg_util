## 0. How to use this document

This file captures the OSU IRB and IEEE publication requirements that govern the sEMG/IMU tennis study. It is the **reference context** when drafting `IRB_Packet_OSU_sEMG_Tennis.md` and the ethics sections of `paper_cnib.md`. Direct quotes from authoritative sources are marked with `>`. Items marked **TBD** require user/PI input before submission.

When IEEE policy and OSU policy collide, OSU policy controls study conduct (it has legal force); IEEE policy controls only what must be in the manuscript.

### 1.7 Data security and privacy

Source: [OSU IRB — New Applications (Data security plan)](https://research.oregonstate.edu/ori/irb/new-applications)

- OSU classifies data into Levels 1–3 (Public / Confidential / High)
- **Level 3** data security plans must be reviewed by the OSU Information Security Office before research starts
- This study collects raw sEMG, IMU, and 0–10 subjective ratings tied to a single named minor each, with no PHI/PII beyond name and code → **likely Level 2 (Confidential)**, not Level 3
- **N=2 indirect-identifiability warning:** because the cohort is two people and both participants may be identifiable through manuscript authorship and study context, the protocol treats raw and coded physiological data as **potentially identifiable even after removal of direct identifiers**. Public sharing of raw participant-level data is **not** promised; data-availability statements (IEEE) reflect this constraint.
- **Level 2 cloud storage caveat:** per OSU guidance, if coded or identifiable Level 2 data are stored on a cloud-based server, the data-security plan must be reviewed by the OSU Information Security Office **unless** the cloud server is licensed by OSU. To avoid triggering additional ISO review, this study will use **OSU-licensed storage or encrypted local storage** with controlled access.
- No video, audio, heart-rate, SpO₂, or other vital-sign data
- Coded storage with the identity-key file held separately on encrypted local drive
- Routine encrypted backup; patched OS; firewall; access limited to named study team only

### 1.8 Subpart D — Children in research

Source: [45 CFR 46 Subpart D (eCFR)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-A/part-46/subpart-D)

- **Risk classification:** [45 CFR 46.404](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-A/part-46/subpart-D/section-46.404) — *Research not involving greater than minimal risk*
- **Permission requirement ([§46.408(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-A/part-46/subpart-D/section-46.408)):**
  - For research approvable under [§46.404](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-A/part-46/subpart-D/section-46.404) (this study) or [§46.405](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-A/part-46/subpart-D/section-46.405), the IRB **may** find that permission of **one parent** is sufficient.
  - For research under [§46.406](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-A/part-46/subpart-D/section-46.406) or [§46.407](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-A/part-46/subpart-D/section-46.407), permission of **both parents** is required unless one parent is deceased, unknown, incompetent, not reasonably available, or only one has legal responsibility for the child.
  - **Study-specific stance:** because both minor participants are children of individuals affiliated with the study team, the protocol will seek written permission from **both parents when feasible** as an additional protection against perceived undue influence — even though the regulation does not require it for §46.404 work. If only one parent signs, the application must document the regulatory basis and the IRB's accepted justification.
- **Assent ([§46.408(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-A/part-46/subpart-D/section-46.408)):** Required from the child where in the IRB's judgment the children are capable of providing assent. Both Ryan (17) and Theodore (15) are capable of providing meaningful written assent.
- **Exemption pathway not used:** although certain Common Rule exemptions can apply to research with children, this study should **not** rely on exempt status because it involves minors, coded/identifiable physiological sensor data, and research procedures during physical activity. The application is framed as **non-exempt minimal-risk research under §46.404**, anticipated eligible for **expedited** review at OSU's discretion.
- **18th-birthday transition:** Re-consent as adult required if data continues to be collected after the participant turns 18; previously-collected data retained under parental permission stays usable

## Part 2 — IEEE Publication Requirements

### 2.1 Verbatim policy text (IEEE Author Center, Submission and Peer Review Policies)

Source: [IEEE — Submission and Peer Review Policies](https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/become-an-ieee-journal-author/publishing-ethics/guidelines-and-policies/submission-and-peer-review-policies/)

**IRB statement requirement** (direct quote):

> "the research was performed under the oversight of an institutional review board or equivalent local/regional body, including the official name of the IRB/ethics committee"

(or, if not reviewed, an explanation of why such review wasn't conducted)

**Consent statement requirement** (direct quote):

> "shall also report that consent from the human subjects in the research was obtained or explain why consent was not obtained"

### 2.2 Authorship criteria (IEEE Ethical Requirements)

Source: [IEEE — Ethical Requirements](https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/become-an-ieee-journal-author/publishing-ethics/ethical-requirements/) · [ICMJE Authorship Criteria](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/roles-and-responsibilities/defining-the-role-of-authors-and-contributors.html)

Authors must meet **all three** criteria, including:

> "made a significant intellectual contribution to the theoretical development, system or experimental design, prototype development"

(plus drafting/revising and final approval / accountability — standard ICMJE-style triad).

Contributors not meeting all three criteria belong in **Acknowledgments**, not the author list.

**Implication for this study:**
- Ryan Wang — qualifies (system design participation, primary data collection, analysis pipeline, drafting) — **lead student author**
- Theodore (Teddy) Di — qualifies via second-site data collection, cross-site reproducibility analysis, and manuscript review — **defined co-author role**
- Dr. YanMing Di — qualifies via study design, statistical oversight, and supervisory role — **PI / corresponding author**
- Xiaodong Wang (Technical Assistant) — does **not** qualify under IEEE criteria because his role is engineering support and on-court supervision, not the manuscript's intellectual content → **Acknowledgments only, not an author**

### 2.3 Conflict of interest

Source: [IEEE — Submission and Peer Review Policies (COI section)](https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/become-an-ieee-journal-author/publishing-ethics/guidelines-and-policies/submission-and-peer-review-policies/)

IEEE definition (paraphrased): any situation, transaction, or relationship in which someone's decisions or actions could materially affect that individual's professional, personal, financial, or business concerns. Reviewers and editors with conflicts must recuse.

**Implication:** the Author COI declaration in the manuscript must disclose:
- Dr. Di is the parent of co-author/participant Theodore
- Xiaodong Wang (in Acknowledgments) is the parent of co-author/participant Ryan
- Independent Assent Administrator details

### 2.4 Plagiarism, originality, prior publication

Source: [IEEE — Ethical Requirements](https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/become-an-ieee-journal-author/publishing-ethics/ethical-requirements/) · [Submission and Peer Review Policies](https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/become-an-ieee-journal-author/publishing-ethics/guidelines-and-policies/submission-and-peer-review-policies/)

- All articles screened for plagiarism before publication
- Submission must be original, not previously published, not under simultaneous review elsewhere
- Conference→journal evolution allowed if the journal version "contains substantially more technical information"; all prior versions must be referenced
- Self-plagiarism (recycled text from author's own prior work) must be marked, referenced, and explained

### 2.5 Data integrity

Source: [IEEE — Ethical Requirements (Data Reporting)](https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/become-an-ieee-journal-author/publishing-ethics/ethical-requirements/)

No fabrication, no falsification, no image manipulation that alters scientific meaning.

### 2.6 AI-generated content disclosure

Source: [IEEE — Submission and Peer Review Policies (AI-Generated Content)](https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/become-an-ieee-journal-author/publishing-ethics/guidelines-and-policies/submission-and-peer-review-policies/)

> Use of AI-generated content (text, figures, images, code) "shall be disclosed in the acknowledgments section."

Disclosure must identify: the AI system used, the specific sections involved, and a brief usage explanation. Editing/grammar tools are exempt but disclosure is recommended.

### 2.7 Preprint posting

Source: [IEEE — Post-Publication Policies](https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/become-an-ieee-journal-author/publishing-ethics/guidelines-and-policies/post-publication-policies/) · [Submission and Peer Review Policies](https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/become-an-ieee-journal-author/publishing-ethics/guidelines-and-policies/submission-and-peer-review-policies/)

Permitted with required boilerplate before acceptance:

> "This work has been submitted to the IEEE for possible publication. Copyright may be transferred without notice, after which this version may no longer be accessible."

### 2.8 Biomedical-venue norms (not core IEEE policy, but expected by EMBC / JBHI / JTEHM / TBME / Sensors)

These are **not** in IEEE's core ethics text but are standard practice and expected by reviewers in biomedical venues:

| Item | Norm | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Declaration of Helsinki | Conformance statement in Methods (e.g., "The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and approved by [IRB name], protocol number [#]") | [WMA Declaration of Helsinki](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-helsinki-ethical-principles-for-medical-research-involving-human-subjects/) |
| Author contributions | CRediT taxonomy or narrative section listing each author's role | [CRediT taxonomy](https://credit.niso.org/) |
| Data availability statement | Required by some IEEE biomedical journals; for this study expect: "Raw data are not publicly available because the IRB-approved protocol does not authorize public sharing of single-participant identifiable physiological data; de-identified summary metrics are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request." | Verify per venue |
| Funding statement | Required even if "This work received no external funding." | Standard |
| ORCID | Compulsory for IEEE Sensors Journal; recommended elsewhere | [ORCID](https://orcid.org) |

## Part 7 — Protocol-drafting checklist (derived from review)

These are not OSU/IEEE *requirements* per se; they are protocol elements that an internal review of this document flagged as worth including in `IRB_Packet_OSU_sEMG_Tennis.md` because they materially strengthen the OSU IRB application or address known reviewer concerns.

### 7.1 Closed-cohort recruitment and no compensation
- No public recruitment; closed N=2 cohort known to study team.
- No compensation, no cost to participants.
- Participation does not affect school standing, tennis opportunities, family relationships, authorship, or other benefits.

### 7.2 Participation–authorship separation
- Authorship and acknowledgment decisions are not contingent on a minor's continued data-collection participation.
- A participant may withdraw from data collection while still receiving appropriate scholarly credit for work already completed, where applicable under publication standards.
- The decision to participate in data collection is documented separately from any manuscript authorship agreement. Refusal or withdrawal from human-subjects participation will **not** be treated as misconduct, failure, or lack of scholarly contribution.

### 7.3 Independent Assent Administrator (IAA) — formal protection
- IAA is **not** a parent, co-author, technical assistant, supervisor, or anyone with a publication interest in the study.
- IAA confirms privately with each minor that participation is voluntary, may be declined or withdrawn without penalty, and is not required to maintain family, athletic, authorship, or academic relationships.

### 7.4 Device safety language (passive-recording only)
- Surface EMG sensors record naturally occurring muscle electrical activity from the skin surface.
- Device is **passive**: no electrical stimulation, no medication, no heat, no therapeutic intervention, battery powered, no diagnostic or treatment claims.
- Participant may remove sensors at any time. Stop on discomfort, skin irritation, dizziness, pain, or request.

### 7.5 No clinical/device-claim framing
- The system is used only for non-clinical research signal recording and engineering analysis.
- Not used for diagnosis, treatment, prevention, monitoring of a medical condition, or clinical decision-making.
- The study does not test a therapeutic or diagnostic device claim. The PI will address any OSU questions about FDA/device applicability if raised, rather than asserting a categorical "not a medical device" determination upfront.
- **No medical feedback to participants.** Participants and parents will not receive medical interpretation, diagnosis, treatment advice, or individualized health recommendations based on the sEMG/IMU data. Tennis coaching or practice decisions remain outside the research protocol.

### 7.6 Ordinary tennis risk vs. research-added risk
- Tennis itself involves ordinary athletic risk (fatigue, soreness, falls, minor injury).
- Research does not require participants to exceed normal practice intensity.
- Research-added risks are limited to possible mild skin irritation, minor inconvenience, and confidentiality risk.

### 7.7 Off-site / private-court privacy
- No public spectators invited by researchers.
- No video or audio recording.
- No school or team involvement; collection at private residential courts only.
- Supervising adult present for safety; collection postponed for unsafe weather/court conditions.

### 7.8 Reportable adverse events / deviations (specifics)
- Skin redness lasting beyond a session.
- Rash or blistering from electrodes, tape, or straps.
- Pain or unusual discomfort from device placement.
- Privacy breach, lost device, or lost data.
- Distress or perceived pressure to participate.
- Any tennis injury during a research-recorded session — even if likely ordinary sport-related — logged and evaluated.

### 7.9 Adult re-consent operational detail
- New data collection pauses at age 18 until adult informed consent is obtained.
- Pre-18 data remain usable under original parental permission and minor assent unless the now-adult participant withdraws.
- If the now-adult participant declines re-consent, no further data collection for that participant.
- **Withdrawal / data-removal limits.** Participants may request withdrawal of identifiable or coded data to the extent practicable. Data that have already been de-identified, aggregated, analyzed, or included in submitted/published results may not be removable. This limit must be disclosed in the parental permission and minor assent forms.

### 7.10 Data retention and future use
- Retention period: e.g., **5 years after publication or study closure**, identity-key file destroyed earlier when no longer needed (final value TBD by PI).
- De-identified aggregate summary metrics may be retained longer if IRB approves.
- Future use: data are used only for the approved study and related publications/presentations described in this IRB application; materially different future use requires IRB amendment.
