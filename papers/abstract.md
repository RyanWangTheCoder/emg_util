# Abstract

Overuse injuries in repetitive sports movements are often preceded by progressive neuromuscular fatigue, yet objective, field-deployable monitoring remains limited by sensor synchronization, motion artifacts, and the need for actionable, personalized feedback. We present an end-to-end wearable sensing and analytics system that continuously captures synchronized surface electromyography (sEMG), inertial measurement unit (IMU), and photoplethysmography-derived vital signals (heart rate and SpO₂) to quantify fatigue and estimate overuse-injury risk during real-world training. The prototype integrates a compact, battery-powered sensor node with robust Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) streaming and a cross-platform data pipeline that supports real-time visualization and long-duration logging on both PC and Android.

Key technical contributions include: (i) multimodal acquisition aligned in device time using high-resolution on-device timestamps to enable reliable sensor fusion over extended sessions; (ii) event-aligned, activity-gated fatigue analysis, where IMU-based segmentation isolates biomechanically meaningful actions (e.g., stroke/swing events) while suppressing rest and non-task motion; and (iii) a hybrid fatigue inference framework that combines established EMG amplitude/spectral indicators with kinematic stability and variability metrics and cardiovascular load signals to provide context-aware fatigue estimates. To support real-world variability, the system incorporates baseline-referenced, per-user adaptation and modular interfaces that extend to additional sensors and multi-muscle configurations. The proposed platform provides a reproducible pathway from raw wearable signals to fatigue features, predictive modeling, and actionable feedback, forming a foundation for longitudinal dataset creation and AI-driven overuse-injury prevention in sports and occupational settings.
